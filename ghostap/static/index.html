      How GhostAP works

      1. Listen. Every Wi-Fi access point announces itself about ten times a second with a beacon: its name, its radio address (BSSID), channel, security, and how it is built. GhostAP only listens; it never sends anything.
      2. Learn normal. For the first 2 minutes GhostAP learns what each network normally looks like: which access points it has, their vendors, channels, security, signal and uptime.
      3. Look for differences. After that, every access point is compared with what was learned, using eight indicators (below). Each gives a value from 0 to 1 and a reason.
      4. Score and smooth. The indicators are combined into a weighted risk score from 0 to 100, averaged over the last 30 seconds so that one odd frame cannot raise an alarm. MEDIUM from 30, HIGH from 60.
      Synthetic test capture: a twin of “MVSR_WiFi” appears after three minutes (about 18 seconds at ×10). Not available during a live capture.

      Why not just look for duplicate names?

      Big networks (campus, office) legitimately use many access points with the same name. A name-only check alarms on all of them. The Name-only tab shows how many alarms that simple approach would raise on this capture.
